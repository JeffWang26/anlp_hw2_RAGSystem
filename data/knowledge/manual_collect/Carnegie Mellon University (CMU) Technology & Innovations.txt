Andrew File System (AFS): The Andrew File System (AFS) is a distributed file system developed at Carnegie Mellon University in the 1980s. It was named after the university's founders, Andrew Carnegie and Andrew Mellon, and was designed to support a vast network of student computing.

Deep Thought: Deep Thought was a chess-playing computer developed by researchers at Carnegie Mellon University in the late 1980s. It was the predecessor to IBM's Deep Blue, which eventually defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov.

Virtual Reality Pioneer: Ivan Sutherland, a pioneer in computer graphics who created the first head-mounted display system for virtual reality, was a professor at Carnegie Mellon University.

NavLab: The NavLab project at Carnegie Mellon University, which began in 1984, was a groundbreaking research initiative that built some of the world's very first autonomous, computer-controlled vehicles.

Libratus: Libratus is an artificial intelligence program developed at Carnegie Mellon University's School of Computer Science that made history by decisively beating top professional human players at No-Limit Texas Hold 'em poker.

CubeRover: CubeRover is a class of lightweight, shoebox-sized planetary rovers developed by Astrobotic Technology in close partnership with students and researchers at Carnegie Mellon University, designed to make lunar exploration more accessible.
