Carnegie Mellon University
Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) was founded in 1900 in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. It was established by Andrew Carnegie as the Carnegie Technical Schools. The original purpose of the Carnegie Technical Schools was to provide vocational training to the sons and daughters of Pittsburgh's working class. In 1912, the Carnegie Technical Schools became the Carnegie Institute of Technology (CIT). In 1967, the Carnegie Institute of Technology merged with the Mellon Institute of Industrial Research to form Carnegie Mellon University. The Mellon Institute was founded by Andrew W. Mellon and Richard B. Mellon.

CMU's motto is "My heart is in the work." Carnegie Mellon University is located in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. CMU comprises seven colleges and schools: the College of Engineering, College of Fine Arts, Dietrich College of Humanities and Social Sciences, Heinz College of Information Systems and Public Policy, Mellon College of Science, School of Computer Science, and Tepper School of Business. The School of Computer Science at Carnegie Mellon is renowned for its computer science program. The Tepper School of Business is Carnegie Mellon's business school.

Notable alumni include Andy Warhol, associated with the Pop Art movement. The student newspaper is The Tartan. The official mascot of Carnegie Mellon University is the Scottish Terrier (Scotty). The official colors of Carnegie Mellon University are plaid (tartan), though they also use black and red. The annual event where students build and race buggies is called Buggy or Sweepstakes. The Fence at Carnegie Mellon University is a campus landmark that students paint. The College of Fine Arts is housed in the College of Fine Arts building. The main library is Hunt Library. Randy Pausch's "Last Lecture" is known for inspiring people to achieve their childhood dreams. Sun Microsystems was co-founded by CMU alumnus Vinod Khosla. The annual spring carnival is called Spring Carnival. Gates Hillman Center has a distinctive cylindrical design. The Kiltie Band is CMU's bagpipe and drum band. The Robotics Institute focuses on robotics research. The BXA program combines technology and the arts. Scott Fahlman created the emoticon :-). Scotty Saturday is a football game day tradition. Warner Hall serves as the main administrative hub. The Mobot competition involves autonomous robots. Zachary Quinto played Spock in the Star Trek reboot. Carnegie Mellon's name combines Andrew Carnegie and the Mellon family. The Cut is a central walkway on campus. The Plaid Parliament of Pwning is CMU's competitive cybersecurity team.

Pittsburgh
Pittsburgh is named after William Pitt, the Prime Minister of Great Britain. Pittsburgh is located at the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers, which form the Ohio River. Pittsburgh is known as the "Steel City" due to its historical steel industry. The Pittsburgh Steelers play at Acrisure Stadium (formerly Heinz Field). The Pittsburgh Pirates are a Major League Baseball team. The Pittsburgh Penguins are a National Hockey League team. Point State Park is at the confluence of the three rivers. The Cathedral of Learning is a landmark at the University of Pittsburgh. Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens is a botanical garden in Pittsburgh. The Carnegie Science Center is a science museum. Andrew Carnegie was a industrialist who had a major impact on Pittsburgh's development. Pittsburgh has many bridges. The Roberto Clemente Bridge is named after the baseball legend. The Strip District is known for food and markets. Wholey's is famous for fish in the Strip District. Pittsburgh's inclines are historic cable cars.
